# The purpose of the game

The players try to build up a company. They issue liabilities (debt) to finance investments in assets. The aim is to maximize the value of the company by maximizing the value of assets while minimizing the cost of liabilities (trade credit, bank loans or bonds). Players take a different role every turn and use the specific characteristics of the roles to achieve this.

# Preparation

The Chairman of the Board-card is given to the oldest player. That player starts as Chairman and puts the card in the card stand in front of them. Then the Chairman shuffles the deck of character cards, the deck of asset cards, the deck of liability cards and the deck of market cards. You can in- or exclude the "event cards" in the latter. The Chairman puts all decks face down on the table with the 30 cash tokens and turns the first market card. Every player now takes one "balance sheet", puts one cash token on it's cash area and takes two cards from the asset and the liability decks on hand. All players may also take one overview card. You are ready to play!

# Asset cards

Assets add value to your company because they generate income (cash flow). To buy an asset, you pay the number of gold circles (book value). The market value of assets changes with changing market conditions. If the market is positive for an asset type (sign in the corresponding circle on the market conditions card is +) you add the silver circles. If it is neutral (sign is 0) the value equals the golden circles. If it is negative (sign is -), you subtract the silver from the golden circles. The number of silver over golden circles thus measures your company value's sensitivity to the market conditions. This we refer to as the company's "beta". Note that more risk can increase cash flow in good market conditions but also implies higher financing risk!

# Liability cards

To buy assets you need to raise capital. You can use your cash income, but you can also issue trade credit, bank loans or bonds to finance your investments in assets. You need to pay interest on these liabilities. Trade credit is cheapest, then bank loans, and bonds are most expensive. Debt is costly, but if you use it to finance the right investments, your company will still be more valuable. So issue liabilities wisely.

# Market Conditions and Event Cards

Market conditions and events (optional) affect both the value of assets and the costs of capital. Whenever any player buys their first, second, third, fourth, fifth, seventh or eighth asset for the first time in the game, a new market card is turned. Market conditions replace the previous market conditions. Events are read out loud, executed and put aside, so they are open at the end of the game. Always turn a new card until one new market conditions card has been opened. There must be a market conditions card open on the table at all times.

This market conditions card shows all relevant information to value all companies' assets and liabilities. For the liabilities it shows the risk-free rate (RFR) and the market risk premium (MRP). For assets it shows market sentiment per color (positive +, neutral 0, negative -).

# Game play for 4-7 players

The Chairman of the Board starts the game. They shuffle the deck of character cards and, depending on the number of players, put 0-2 character cards open on the table.

For 4 players, open 2 cards
For 5 players, open 1 card
For 6-7 players, no cards open

Is the CEO among the open cards, then put it back in the stack and replace it with the next card. After shuffling the remaining characters, the Chairman of the Board looks at the first character card and puts that, unseen by the other players, on the table (NOTE also if that card is the CEO).

From the remaining characters, the Chairman can choose one and place it face down in front of them. They then give the remaining characters to the player to their left. The next players do the same, until the last player gets the card(s) that are left. This last player takes the closed character the Chairman put on the table and then chooses their character. They put the remaining character(s) unseen on the table. Make sure the Chairman of the Board does not know what character you chose!

# The Boardmeeting

Then, the board meeting is in session. The acting Chairman of the Board calls the roll in the indicated order. If a character is called, the player with that card reveals themselves and puts their character card open in front of them. If no player responds, because the character is open or closed on the table, the next character is called. A character that was fired by the shareholder is skipped. When all 8 characters have been called, the next round begins.

When a character is called, the player that chose it reveals their character card and starts their turn. They first receive one cash token. Then, in any order:

- Take three cards from the assets and/or the liabilities stack on hand and choose two cards from the three cards to keep and return the third.
- Buy one asset and pay the indicated amount from cash.
- Issue one liability and receive the indicated amount of cash.

Use your character's special characteristic. Your character may imply you can buy more assets, issue more liabilities or take more cards in the actions above, as well as earn income on assets.

Note: Every action is optional but can be taken only once per turn.

# The 8 characters

1. The shareholder can fire any character except the banker and the regulator. The character the shareholder chooses to fire does not reveal themselves, but they will be skipped in this round of the game. A fired character skips their turn in silence.

2. The banker can terminate the credit line to any character, except the shareholder and the regulator. When called, the chosen character can take one cash but must then pay one cash plus one for every color of assets they have on their balance sheet to the banker. If they have too little cash, they must sell assets at market value to raise cash. The CFO can issue liabilities first, to raise cash.

3. The regulator can trade their hand cards (including an empty hand) with any of the other players or put any number of cards at the bottom of the deck and draw the same number of new cards from the top.

4. The Chief Executive Officer, CEO, is given the Chairman of the Board card and acts as Chairman of the Board from the next round on. The CEO earns one cash for each yellow asset on their balance sheet, plus or minus one if the market conditions card shows a + or - in the yellow circle, respectively. The CEO can buy up to three new assets in their turn.

5. The Chief Financial Officer, CFO, earns one cash for each blue asset on their balance sheet, plus or minus one if the market conditions card shows a + or - in the blue circle, respectively. The CFO can issue or redeem up to three liabilities in their turn. If required to terminate the credit line by the banker, the CFO may issue liabilities from his hand to raise the cash.

6. The Chief Sustainability Officer, CSO, earns one cash for each green asset on their balance sheet, plus or minus one if the market conditions card shows a + or - in the green circle, respectively. The CSO can buy up to two red and/or green assets and cannot be forced to disinvest by the Stakeholder.

7. The Head of R&D, earns one cash for each purple asset on their balance sheet, plus or minus one if the market conditions card shows a + or - in the purple circle, respectively. The head of R&D can draw six cards from the assets or liabilities stack and put back only two.

8. The stakeholder earns one cash for each red asset on their balance sheet, plus or minus one if the market conditions card shows a + or - in the red circle, respectively. The stakeholder can force another player (not the CSO) to divest from any asset (not red or green) at market value minus one. The divested asset is returned to the stack.

Three examples illustrate the use of the characters.

The regulator has two cash on hand and a four-gold yellow asset on hand. They get one cash and issue a two-gold bank loan, purchase the four-gold asset and trades their now empty hand with a player that has three cards on hand. At the end of their turn, the regulator now has three cards on hand and the player with whom they traded holds zero.

The CEO recieves one cash and issues a one-gold bond, taking two cash on hand. He has two yellow assets on the balance sheet and one yellow asset of three gold and one green asset for one gold on hand. Because market conditions card shows a 0 in the yellow circle, they first receive two cash income for the two yellow assets. Then they purchase the three-gold yellow asset and the one-gold green asset and put these on the asset side of the balance sheet. The CEO cannot take income for the newly bought asset(s).

The CFO has one cash and a two-gold bond at RFR plus 3% on their balance sheet and two bank loans for one and two gold at RFR plus 2% and a red asset for three gold on hand. The CFO recieves one cash and issues the two bank loans for three cash total. They now have five cash on the balance sheet and can purchase the three-gold red asset on hand and redeem the two-gold bond.

# End of the game and the winner

The game ends when at least one player has 6 (or more) assets on their balance sheet. The round is completed and all characters that have not yet been called, can take their last turn. Note that the game also ends if the stakeholder forces the winner to divest and no player ends the round with 6 assets.

# End of Game Cash Bonusses:

Players may:
Add 5 cash when they have assets of all 5 colors.
Add 4 cash for the player that reaches 6 (or more) assets first.
Add 2 cash for the players that hold 6 (or more) assets after the last round.

Do not forget to use the special features of your purple assets and any open events cards!

# The value of the business can then be computed.

In increasing order of complexity, you can use any one of the methods below. You want to agree on the method before playing the game:

1. Quick and Dirty: Compute the market value of assets by adding, ignoring or subtracting the silver from the gold circles on all asset cards in a bull, neutral and bear market, respectively. Then add cash and subtract total debt.
2. Quick: Compute the market value of assets by adding, ignoring or subtracting the silver from the gold circles on asset cards by color when the market conditions card reveals a +, 0 or -, respectively, in the corresponding color circles. Then add cash and subtract total debt.
3. Present Values: Compute the present value of expected cash flows by computing the market value of assets as under 2. Assuming an average payback time of 10 years, divide this number by 10 to compute the expected annual cash flow. Then divide this number by the risk free rate (RFR) plus your company’s beta (total silver divided by total gold) times the market risk premium (MRP) on the market conditions card to obtain the present value of expected cash flows (PVECF). To compute the present value of debt liabilities (PVDL), take the sum of all debt and divide that by the risk free rate (RFR) plus the average debt risk premium (DRP = (TC+(2xBL)+(3xBD))/D). Then add cash to the PVECF and subtract the PVDL.
